# Lunar phase and voluntary fasting reminders

Research for [How should lunar phase and voluntary fasting dates be distinguished?](https://github.com/Today20092/OpenMasjidDisplay/issues/12), checked 18 September 2026. This report supplies evidence and product recommendations, not an approved calendar policy or personal religious instruction.

## Findings

The White Days use the 13th, 14th and 15th dates of the Hijri month. The translated report of Abu Dharr specifies those three dates in [Jami at-Tirmidhi 761](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:761), graded Hasan on the cited edition. It does not specify an illumination threshold. [NASA's moon-phase explanation](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-phases/) describes full moon as an astronomical phase and distinguishes waxing from waning. Consequently, deriving White Days from a percentage, or marking three days around the astronomical full moon, would substitute a different rule. Keep the moon graphic, illuminated percentage and next full-moon date separate from the calendar's White Days status. This separation is a product inference from those sources.

[Jami at-Tirmidhi 747](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:747), also graded Hasan on the cited edition, records fasting in connection with Monday and Thursday. This supports the requested optional advance reminders. The choice to display them on Sunday and Wednesday, and their duration or wording, is a product choice rather than a timing rule supplied by that report.

Hijri dates need an explicit calendar basis. The [US Naval Observatory's calendar explanation](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/islamic) distinguishes a sighting-based calendar from a tabular calendar. Its [observance service](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/data/islamic_observances) warns that its results are tabular dates while observed religious dates depend on crescent visibility. A computed date therefore cannot silently stand for every mosque's adopted date. Let the installer select or confirm the calendar used by that community and correct its dates when needed. Selection mechanism, available calendars and correction scope remain decisions; these sources do not establish one universal method for all communities.

The same [USNO observance explanation](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/data/islamic_observances) says its Hijri dates run from the preceding sunset to sunset on the tabulated civil date. This differs from the agreed midnight rollover for the daily prayer schedule. On Sunday evening, the Hijri date can already describe Monday's daylight while the civil date still says Sunday. A reminder should explicitly name the coming daylight date, for example "Monday fasting reminder · tomorrow, 21 September", instead of deriving "tomorrow" by incrementing a Hijri date after sunset. This is a recommended display convention, not a change to the agreed prayer schedule rollover.

## Exceptions to blanket invitations

The sources rule out treating every matching weekday or monthly date as an unconditional invitation:

- [Sahih al-Bukhari 1990](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1990) records the prohibition of fasting on Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha.
- [Sunan Ibn Majah 1720](https://sunnah.com/ibnmajah:1720) identifies Tashriq as 11, 12 and 13 Dhu al-Hijjah and describes these as days for eating and drinking. [Sahih al-Bukhari 1997–1998](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1997) records a specific pilgrimage exception. Thus even the 13th can overlap a date unsuitable for a generic voluntary-fast invitation; the display should not infer an individual's eligibility.
- [Sahih al-Bukhari 8](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:8) identifies Ramadan fasting among Islam's foundations. A Ramadan date should not be presented as merely an optional Monday, Thursday or White Days fast.

Recommended product handling, awaiting approval: suppress generic voluntary-fast invitations on the selected calendar's Eid and Tashriq dates and during Ramadan. Continue displaying factual moon phase and calendar information. Do not automatically invent replacement fasting days or decide pilgrimage exceptions. Any broader exception policy requires the community's chosen guidance; this report is not an exhaustive jurisprudence survey.

## Smallest proposed behavior

Keep astronomical moon data independent of the selected Hijri calendar. Put optional reminders in a quiet area without replacing prayer information. Use a named daylight date for advance reminders and apply exception checks to that target date, including when it crosses a month boundary. When the calendar has not been confirmed, show astronomical data without asserting fasting eligibility. Merge overlapping Monday/Thursday and White Days reminders into one notice.

Pending human decisions are the calendar selection and correction method, sunset label behavior, exception policy, lead time for approaching White Days, and wording. Later acceptance checks should cover Sunday before and after sunset, midnight, Hijri month changes, the 13th of Dhu al-Hijjah, Ramadan overlaps, and a near-full moon that is not a White Day in the selected calendar.
